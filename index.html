Tour stop 01

A trip through the eras

See how gaming grew from simple arcade machines to the worlds we play in today.

1971 to 1979

Insert coin to begin

It started with a bleep. In the 1970s, games lived in wooden cabinets at pizza parlors, bowling alleys, and corner arcades. A pocket full of quarters and three initials on the high-score table were all anyone needed.

  • 1972 Pong starts bleeping in bars and lounges
  • 1977 The Atari 2600 brings the arcade home
  • 1978 Space Invaders lines up the first big crowds

1980 to 1989

Press start

The 8-bit decade gave us plumbers, ghosts, and tunes you can still hum today. Games moved from the arcade to the living room carpet, and Saturday mornings were never the same.

  • 1980 Pac-Man becomes the first true gaming icon
  • 1985 The NES arrives in North America
  • 1989 The Game Boy puts it all in your pocket

1990 to 1999

Blowing on cartridges, spinning up discs

Sixteen bits of color, then a leap into full 3D. The 90s brought rival consoles, playground debates, memory cards, and the first time a game world felt big enough to get lost in.

  • 1991 The Super NES lands in North America
  • 1995 The PlayStation spins up its first discs
  • 1996 The Nintendo 64 brings analog sticks and 3D worlds

2000 to 2009

Couch co-op and going online

Sleepovers turned into marathons, LAN parties took over basements, and for the first time you could play with friends across town. Then motion controls got grandparents bowling in the living room.

  • 2000 The PlayStation 2 becomes a living-room staple
  • 2001 Xbox and GameCube join the party
  • 2006 The Wii gets everyone off the couch

2010 to today

Worlds without edges

Huge open worlds, tiny indie gems, and consoles you can take on the bus. Today's games stand on the shoulders of every bleep and bloop that came before, which is exactly why the classics are worth remembering.

  • 2013 A new console generation goes digital-first
  • 2017 The Nintendo Switch blurs home and handheld
  • 2020 Lightning-fast loading makes waiting a memory

The machines under the TV

Released 1977

Atari 2600

The wood-grain box that brought the arcade home. One joystick, one red button, and a stack of chunky cartridges were enough to keep the whole family up way past bedtime.

Released 1985 (North America)

Nintendo Entertainment System

After the video game crash of 1983, this gray box put games back under the TV. Blowing into the cartridges never actually helped, but every single one of us did it anyway.
